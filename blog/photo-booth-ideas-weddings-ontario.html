Photo Booth Ideas for Weddings in Ontario: Themes, Props & Backdrops

May 20, 2025 • 6 min read · By YM Event Vendors

A wedding photo booth is only as memorable as the thought put into it. From backdrops to props to custom overlays, here are the best ideas Ontario couples are using in 2026 to make their photo booth a highlight of the night.

Start with the backdrop

The backdrop is the first thing guests see and the background of every photo — so it's worth choosing thoughtfully. Here are the most popular backdrop options for Ontario weddings right now:

Flower walls are the perennial favourite. A lush floral backdrop in white, blush, or your wedding colours looks beautiful in photos, photographs exceptionally well with any lighting, and suits everything from rustic barn weddings to upscale venue receptions.

Sequin shimmer backdrops are perfect for evening receptions where you want something glam and high-energy. The sequins catch light beautifully and give every photo a celebratory, polished feel. Gold, silver, rose gold, and champagne are the most popular colours.

Greenery and botanical walls have grown in popularity alongside the garden wedding trend. A lush green leaf wall feels fresh, organic, and works beautifully outdoors or in naturally lit spaces.

Custom printed backdrops are a great option for couples who want something truly one-of-a-kind — a backdrop printed with your monogram, a pattern that matches your wedding stationery, or a design that captures something meaningful about you as a couple.

Prop ideas that guests actually love

The best props are the ones guests reach for naturally. Here's what works well at Ontario weddings:

Speech bubble signs with phrases like "We said yes," "Finally married," "Best day ever," and "About to party" give guests something to hold that adds humour without being too costume-heavy. Custom signs with your wedding hashtag or names are an easy upgrade.

Oversized glasses and hats are classics for a reason — they're universally funny, fit everyone, and photograph well. Keep them clean and in good condition; props that look worn or cheap pull down the quality of every photo.

Seasonal props can be a really fun touch. For a fall Ontario wedding, think flannel shirts, apple baskets, and leaf garlands. For a winter wedding, cozy scarves, toques, and snowflake signs. For summer, sunglasses, tropical flowers, and "just married" signs.

Couple-specific props are the most memorable option. Work with your photo booth provider to create custom props that reflect something personal — your shared hobbies, the place you got engaged, a favourite inside joke. These end up being the photos guests remember and frame.

Custom overlay ideas

Every package from Your Majesty Event Vendors includes a custom digital overlay — the design that gets applied to every photo or video capture. Here's how couples are making the most of it:

Monogram overlays are elegant and timeless. Your initials framed with a simple botanical or geometric design look beautiful on any photo and feel genuinely personal without being busy.

Hashtag overlays are practical as well as decorative. Including your wedding hashtag in the overlay means every photo shared on social media is tagged and easy to find — great for collecting all your wedding content in one place after the event.

Themed overlay designs can match your invitation suite, table settings, or any other visual element from your wedding. If your wedding has a distinctive colour palette or motif — wildflowers, geometric shapes, watercolour textures — we can reflect that in the overlay design.

Booth placement tips

Where you place the photo booth makes a big difference in how much it gets used. The best spot is somewhere highly visible and naturally in the flow of guest traffic — near the bar, beside the dance floor, or in the foyer where guests pass on their way in and out. Avoid tucking it in a corner or behind a partition where guests might not notice it.

If you're having a cocktail hour in a separate room from the reception, consider setting up the booth in the cocktail space first and then moving it to the reception once dancing starts — or running it in one consistent location for the whole night so guests know where to find it.
